# FrederictonHolograms Pilot Partner Brief

## Invitation

FrederictonHolograms is seeking one Fredericton-area museum, library, school,
tourism site, community centre, or innovation space to host a 90-day
Safe AI Learning Station pilot.

The pilot would test an accessible, staff-supported holographic AI learning
experience using clearly fictional AI characters, local cultural content,
and safe AI learning activities.

This is an invitation to explore a practical prototype—not a commitment to a
large deployment, a national program, or guaranteed outcomes.

## What we are proposing

The Safe AI Learning Station would help visitors:

- Explore local history and culture
- Learn how AI works and where it can fail
- Create stories, ideas, or exhibit concepts with AI
- Protect privacy and personal information
- Practise checking AI-generated information
- Participate in accessible creative learning

The station supports staff and volunteers. It does not replace them.

## What is included

FrederictonHolograms would provide:

- A holographic or projected character station
- Two or three safe AI learning activities
- Clearly fictional character interfaces
- Local story or exhibit module template
- Staff facilitation guide
- Accessibility checklist
- Consent and privacy notices
- Feedback and evaluation forms
- Basic technical support during the pilot
- A monthly review report

## What the host provides

A pilot host would provide:

- A suitable physical space
- Access to visitors or participants
- A staff or volunteer contact
- Local context and content review
- A location for the station
- Basic power and internet access, where available
- Feedback from staff and participants

No personal accounts, purchases, or sensitive visitor data are required.

## Safety approach

All AI characters are clearly fictional. They do not claim to be human,
conscious, or an authority.

The station includes:

- Clear AI labels
- Consent before saving or displaying visitor creations
- No required personal accounts
- No impersonation of real people without documented permission
- Source links or references for important historical claims
- Human staff escalation for concerns or accessibility needs
- No medical, legal, or financial advice
- Accessibility options including plain language, captions, large text,
  audio alternatives, and quiet-mode settings

## Who should be involved

The pilot is designed to be co-designed with the people it serves.

We invite participation from:

- Museum and heritage staff
- Librarians and educators
- People with disabilities and accessibility advisors
- Indigenous knowledge holders, where appropriate and invited
- Newcomers and multilingual community members
- Youth and older adults
- Local artists, historians, and creators

Community contributors would help review stories, language, accessibility,
and cultural accuracy.

## 90-day pilot

The pilot would begin with one station, one host organization, and a limited
set of activities.

| Week | Focus |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | Partner agreement, space review, accessibility review, content selection |
| 3–4 | Character and content module development |
| 5–6 | Staff orientation and accessibility testing |
| 7–10 | Public or participant sessions |
| 11–12 | Feedback review, evaluation, and pilot report |

## What we measure

| Measure | Example target |
|---|---:|
| Sessions completed | 30 in 90 days |
| Unique participants | 150 in 90 days |
| Accessibility satisfaction | At least 80% positive |
| Staff-rated usefulness | At least 70% positive |
| Critical content errors | Zero unresolved |
| Consent compliance | 100% |
| Privacy incidents | Zero unresolved |
| Community co-design participation | At least 3 contributors |
| Cost per session | Calculated after pilot |

These are pilot targets, not guarantees.

## What the host receives

A pilot host would receive:

- A documented pilot plan
- Staff orientation materials
- Access to the learning activities and character interface
- A monthly review report
- A final pilot summary
- A clear record of feedback, accessibility findings, and recommended improvements

The host keeps ownership of its own space, visitor relationships, and
approved local content.

## What we do not claim

This pilot does not claim that holograms or AI will solve museum funding,
staffing, attendance, or climate challenges.

It asks a narrower question:

> Can an accessible, staff-supported holographic AI learning station improve
> visitor engagement, staff capacity, cultural interpretation, and community
> participation in a Fredericton pilot?

The answer must come from evidence, not assumption.

## Next step

We are looking for one organization willing to explore a small, clearly
bounded 90-day pilot.

The first step is a short conversation to review:

- Space and visitor access
- Staff availability
- Accessibility needs
- Local content opportunities
- Safety and consent requirements
- A realistic pilot scope
