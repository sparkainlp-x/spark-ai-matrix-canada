# Spark AI Matrix Country Prototype Extension

## Purpose

This extension defines a Country Prototype Framework for safe AI learning,
culturally co-designed holographic characters, and community AI stations.

It turns the Fredericton Safe AI Learning Station pilot into a repeatable
model that can be tested locally, reviewed independently, and expanded only
after evidence shows value, safety, accessibility, and operational feasibility.

This is a research and development framework. It does not claim that the
prototype has been validated, deployed nationally, or proven to produce
measurable national outcomes.

## Country prototype definition

A Country Prototype is a small, well-documented implementation of the Spark AI
Matrix approach in one defined community or region.

It includes:

- A physical or hybrid AI learning station
- Clearly fictional AI characters
- Local cultural and historical content
- Accessibility-first interaction
- Community co-design
- Consent and privacy controls
- Staff facilitation and escalation pathways
- Evaluation measures
- Review gates before expansion

The first prototype is proposed for Fredericton, New Brunswick.

## Fredericton as Pilot Region 1

Fredericton is proposed as Pilot Region 1 because it provides a manageable
local setting for testing the model before any wider deployment.

Potential hosts include museums, libraries, community centres, tourism sites,
schools, and innovation spaces.

The pilot should begin with one station, one host organization, and a limited
set of learning activities.

## Safe AI learning characters

All characters are fictional unless explicit permission, consent, and
appropriate cultural authority are obtained for a real person or community
representative.

| Character | Role | Safety boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Sparky the Beaver | AI-literacy guide | Clearly fictional; does not claim consciousness or authority |
| The Memory Keeper | Local-history guide | Links historical claims to sources |
| The Accessibility Companion | Inclusion guide | Does not diagnose or replace human support |
| The Creator Companion | Creative collaborator | Labels AI output and protects visitor ownership |
| The Question Asker | Critical-thinking guide | Encourages verification and identifies uncertainty |

Every character must clearly state that it is an AI character, not a real
person.

## Accessibility and inclusion

Accessibility is a core requirement, not an optional feature.

Each station should include:

- Plain-language content
- Captions and transcripts
- Large-text and high-contrast options
- Audio alternatives
- Keyboard and screen-reader-friendly digital content
- Physical access review
- Quiet or low-stimulation options
- Staff or volunteer support
- Clear ways to report barriers

People with disabilities, Indigenous knowledge holders, newcomers, youth,
older adults, and local creators should be invited as co-designers—not only
as end users.

## Cultural authority and consent

Cultural content must be reviewed by appropriate community members or
rights holders before publication.

The framework requires:

- Clear ownership and permission records
- Consent for personal stories, images, voices, and creations
- Attribution for community knowledge and contributions
- A process to withdraw or correct content
- No impersonation of real people without documented permission
- No use of sacred, private, or sensitive material without explicit authority

## Station architecture

A minimum viable station includes:

1. Display or projection system
2. Local content module
3. Character interface
4. Accessibility settings
5. Privacy and consent screen
6. Source-linking or reference layer
7. Staff escalation button or process
8. Feedback and evaluation form
9. Offline or low-connectivity fallback
10. Maintenance and incident log

## Local content pipeline

Content should follow a transparent workflow:

1. Identify a local theme, artifact, story, or community need
2. Invite relevant community co-designers
3. Draft content and learning activities
4. Review for accuracy, cultural authority, accessibility, and safety
5. Obtain required permissions and consents
6. Publish with clear labels and sources
7. Collect feedback
8. Revise, retire, or expand the module

## Evaluation and review gates

The prototype should be evaluated before expansion.

| Measure | Example target |
|---|---:|
| Sessions completed | 30 in 90 days |
| Unique participants | 150 in 90 days |
| Accessibility satisfaction | At least 80% positive |
| Staff-rated usefulness | At least 70% positive |
| Critical content errors | Zero unresolved |
| Consent compliance | 100% |
| Privacy incidents | Zero unresolved |
| Cost per session | Calculated after pilot |
| Community co-design participation | At least 3 contributors |

Expansion should proceed only if the review gate shows that the prototype is
safe, accessible, useful, financially viable, and supported by the host
organization and community participants.

## Staged expansion model

| Stage | Scope | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Stage 1: Local pilot | One station, one host, limited content | Complete evaluation and review |
| Stage 2: Paired pilots | Two stations or two host sites | Compare results and improve materials |
| Stage 3: Regional network | Several vetted sites | Standardize training, safety, and evaluation |
| Stage 4: Country prototype | Documented national reference model | Independent review and reproducible materials |

No stage should proceed automatically. Each stage requires evidence, review,
and a renewed decision to continue.

## Risks and claims discipline

The framework must avoid overclaiming.

It should not claim that AI characters are conscious, sentient, therapeutic,
or authoritative. It should not claim national impact before independent
evaluation. It should not present synthetic projections as forecasts.

The appropriate claim is:

> This is a prototype framework for testing safe, accessible, community-led
> AI learning experiences in a defined local setting.
