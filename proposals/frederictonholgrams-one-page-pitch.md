# FrederictonHolograms One-Page Pitch

## Safe AI Learning Through Holographic Characters

**A Fredericton-first prototype for museums, libraries, schools, tourism
sites, and community spaces.**

## The opportunity

Museums, libraries, and community organizations face growing pressure to
engage audiences, support digital literacy, respond to AI, and make cultural
content more accessible.

At the same time, many organizations have limited staff capacity, uneven
digital tools, and uncertainty about how to use AI safely.

FrederictonHolograms proposes a practical response: an accessible,
staff-supported holographic AI learning station.

## The solution

The **Safe AI Learning Station** uses clearly fictional AI characters to help
visitors:

- Explore local history and culture
- Learn how AI works and where it can fail
- Create stories, ideas, or exhibit concepts with AI
- Protect privacy and personal information
- Practise checking AI-generated information
- Participate in accessible creative learning

The station supports staff and volunteers. It does not replace them.

## Example characters

| Character | Role | Safety boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Sparky the Beaver | AI-literacy guide | Clearly fictional; does not claim consciousness or authority |
| The Memory Keeper | Local-history guide | Links historical claims to sources |
| The Accessibility Companion | Inclusion guide | Does not diagnose or replace human support |
| The Creator Companion | Creative collaborator | Labels AI output and protects visitor ownership |
| The Question Asker | Critical-thinking guide | Encourages verification and identifies uncertainty |

## Safe learning activities

- **Ask the Character:** Visitors ask a safe, pre-approved question about a
  local exhibit or story.
- **Spot the Mistake:** Visitors identify a deliberate error in an AI-generated
  explanation.
- **Create With AI:** Visitors create a story, poem, title, or visual concept
  with clear AI labelling.
- **Privacy Mirror:** Visitors learn what information should not be shared
  with an AI system.
- **Accessibility Challenge:** Visitors test whether content works with plain
  language, captions, large text, audio, and screen-reader-friendly formats.

## Safety by design

The station includes:

- Clear labels that characters are fictional AI
- Consent before saving or displaying visitor creations
- No required personal accounts
- No impersonation of real people without documented permission
- Source links or references for important historical claims
- Human staff escalation for concerns or accessibility needs
- No medical, legal, or financial advice
- Plain language, captions, large-text options, audio alternatives, and
  quiet-mode settings

## Fredericton pilot

We propose a 90-day pilot with one host organization, one station, and a
limited set of learning activities.

Potential hosts include:

- Museums and heritage sites
- Public libraries
- Community centres
- Tourism visitor centres
- Schools and adult-learning programs
- Innovation hubs

### What we measure

| Measure | Example target |
|---|---:|
| Sessions completed | 30 in 90 days |
| Unique participants | 150 in 90 days |
| Accessibility satisfaction | At least 80% positive |
| Staff-rated usefulness | At least 70% positive |
| Critical content errors | Zero unresolved |
| Consent compliance | 100% |
| Privacy incidents | Zero unresolved |
| Community co-design participation | At least 3 contributors |
| Cost per session | Calculated after pilot |

These are pilot targets, not guarantees.

## What partners receive

A pilot partner receives:

- A documented pilot plan
- Staff orientation materials
- Access to learning activities and character interfaces
- A monthly review report
- A final pilot summary
- A clear record of accessibility findings, feedback, and improvements

The host retains ownership of its space, visitor relationships, and approved
local content.

## What we do not claim

This proposal does not claim that holograms or AI will solve museum funding,
staffing, attendance, or climate challenges.

It asks a narrower question:

> Can an accessible, staff-supported holographic AI learning station improve
> visitor engagement, staff capacity, cultural interpretation, and community
> participation in a Fredericton pilot?

The answer must come from evidence, not assumption.

## Partnership ask

We are seeking:

- One Fredericton host organization
- A small community co-design group
- A space for a 90-day pilot
- Feedback from staff, visitors, and accessibility advisors
- A conversation about a realistic pilot scope
