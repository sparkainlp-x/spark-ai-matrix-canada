# Safe AI Learning Through Holographic Characters

## Conference proposal

This proposal offers an interactive conference session, workshop, panel, or
demonstration on safe AI learning through holographic characters.

It presents a Fredericton-first prototype designed for museums, libraries,
schools, tourism sites, and community spaces.

## Working title

**Safe AI Learning Through Holographic Characters: A Community Prototype for
Museums, Libraries, and Public Spaces**

## Format options

- 20-minute conference talk
- 45-minute interactive workshop
- 60-minute panel plus live demonstration
- Poster or demonstration station
- Hybrid in-person and online session

## Audience

This session is designed for:

- Cultural-sector workers
- Accessibility advocates
- Community organizers
- Technologists and designers
- Policy and public-engagement professionals
- Students and researchers

## Core message

AI should not only be something done *to* communities. It can be something
communities learn to question, create with, and help govern—through
accessible, locally grounded experiences.

## Abstract

Museums, libraries, and community spaces are under pressure to engage
audiences, support digital literacy, and respond to rapid changes in AI.
This session presents a Fredericton-first prototype: an accessible holographic
AI learning station using clearly fictional characters, local cultural
content, and staff-supported learning activities.

Rather than presenting AI as an authority, the station helps visitors ask
questions, identify errors, protect privacy, and create responsibly. The
session will share the proposed safety framework, accessibility approach,
cultural co-design process, evaluation measures, and honest limitations of
the prototype.

Participants will leave with a practical model for testing safe AI learning
experiences in museums, libraries, schools, and community settings.

## Learning outcomes

Participants will learn how to:

- Design AI learning experiences that are accessible and inclusive
- Use fictional characters safely in cultural and educational settings
- Build consent, privacy, and source-verification practices
- Involve communities as co-designers rather than only users
- Evaluate a small pilot before expanding it
- Avoid overclaiming the capabilities or impact of AI

## Character examples

| Character | Role | Safety boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Sparky the Beaver | AI-literacy guide | Clearly fictional; does not claim consciousness or authority |
| The Memory Keeper | Local-history guide | Links historical claims to sources |
| The Accessibility Companion | Inclusion guide | Does not diagnose or replace human support |
| The Creator Companion | Creative collaborator | Labels AI output and protects visitor ownership |
| The Question Asker | Critical-thinking guide | Encourages verification and identifies uncertainty |

## Safety approach

All AI characters are clearly fictional. They do not claim to be human,
conscious, or an authority.

The proposed station includes:

- Clear AI labels
- Consent before saving or displaying visitor creations
- No required personal accounts
- No impersonation of real people without documented permission
- Source links or references for important historical claims
- Human staff escalation for concerns or accessibility needs
- No medical, legal, or financial advice
- Accessibility options including plain language, captions, large text,
  audio alternatives, and quiet-mode settings

## Accessibility plan

The session and demonstration will use:

- Plain-language slides and handouts
- Captions for any video content
- Large-text and high-contrast options
- Audio alternatives where available
- Keyboard-accessible digital materials
- A quiet or low-stimulation option where feasible
- Clear ways to ask questions or request support
- Advance notice of sensory or accessibility considerations

## Technical requirements

For an in-person demonstration:

- One table or small display area
- Access to power
- A screen, projector, monitor, or holographic display
- Internet access, if available
- Space for 10–30 participants
- A staff or volunteer contact at the venue

The demonstration can also run in an offline or low-connectivity mode using
pre-approved content.

## Evaluation

The conference session can collect optional feedback on:

- Clarity of the safety framework
- Accessibility of the demonstration
- Usefulness for the participant’s organization
- Interest in a local pilot
- Concerns or missing considerations

No personal data will be required. Feedback will be anonymous unless a
participant chooses to share contact information.

## Claims discipline

This proposal does not claim that holograms or AI will solve museum funding,
staffing, attendance, or climate challenges.

It proposes a small, measurable pilot to test whether accessible AI learning
can support visitor engagement, staff capacity, cultural interpretation, and
community participation.
