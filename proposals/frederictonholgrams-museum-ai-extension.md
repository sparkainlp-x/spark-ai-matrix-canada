# FrederictonHolograms Museum and Community AI Learning Extension

## Purpose

This extension proposes a Fredericton-first pilot that connects the Spark AI
Matrix Country Prototype Framework with FrederictonHolograms.

The pilot would create an accessible, staff-supported holographic AI learning
station for museums, libraries, schools, tourism sites, and community spaces.

The purpose is not to replace museum staff or claim that AI solves structural
challenges. The purpose is to test whether safe, accessible, locally grounded
AI learning experiences can support visitor engagement, staff capacity,
cultural interpretation, and community participation.

## Problem

Museums and cultural organizations face overlapping pressures:

- Limited funding and rising operating costs
- Staff capacity constraints
- Uneven digital capability and technical support
- Low visibility for parts of collections that are not on display
- Climate-related risks to facilities and collections
- Growing public interest in AI, alongside uncertainty about safe use
- Accessibility barriers for visitors with diverse cognitive and physical needs

These problems require practical, community-centred tools—not promises that
technology will solve them automatically.

## Proposed solution

### FrederictonHolograms Safe AI Learning Station

The Safe AI Learning Station is a compact holographic or projected learning
experience designed for museums, libraries, schools, and community centres.

It combines:

- A holographic display or projection station
- A small set of clearly fictional AI characters
- Safe AI learning activities
- Accessibility-first interaction
- Human-led facilitation
- Local cultural and historical content
- Consent, privacy, and content-review processes
- Simple evaluation tools

The station supports staff and volunteers. It does not replace them.

## Character approach

All AI characters are clearly fictional. They do not impersonate real people
unless explicit permission, consent, and appropriate cultural authority are
in place.

| Character | Role | What it does | Safety boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sparky the Beaver | AI-literacy guide | Explains how AI works and invites reflection | Clearly fictional; does not claim consciousness or authority |
| The Memory Keeper | Local-history guide | Helps visitors explore Fredericton and New Brunswick stories | Links historical claims to sources |
| The Accessibility Companion | Inclusion guide | Offers plain-language and alternative-format support | Does not diagnose or replace human support |
| The Creator Companion | Creative collaborator | Helps visitors create stories, ideas, or exhibit concepts | Labels AI output and protects visitor ownership |
| The Question Asker | Critical-thinking guide | Helps visitors identify bias, errors, and missing context | Encourages verification |

Every character states that it is an AI character, not a real person.

## Learning activities

### Ask the Character

Visitors ask a safe, pre-approved question about a local exhibit, artifact,
or historical theme.

The character gives a short response, identifies uncertainty, and invites the
visitor to check the museum label, QR code, archive, or staff member.

### Spot the Mistake

The character provides a short local-history explanation with one clearly
marked error. Visitors use exhibit labels or staff support to identify it.

This teaches that AI can be wrong and that verification is important.

### Create With AI

Visitors create a short story, poem, exhibit title, or visual concept
inspired by an artifact or local theme.

All AI-assisted creations are labelled. Visitors decide whether to save,
share, or delete their work.

### Privacy Mirror

Visitors learn what information should not be shared with an AI system,
including personal details, location, passwords, images of other people, and
sensitive family or health information.

### Accessibility Challenge

Visitors test whether an exhibit explanation works with large text, captions,
audio description, plain language, and screen-reader-friendly QR content.

## Safety rules

1. AI characters are fictional.
2. Visitors can stop, skip, repeat, or exit at any time.
3. No personal account is required.
4. No visitor creation, image, voice, or story is saved without consent.
5. Real historical figures are not impersonated without documented permission.
6. Important claims link to sources or museum-approved materials.
7. A staff member or trained volunteer is available for escalation.
8. Accessibility is built into the default experience.
9. Characters do not provide medical, legal, or financial advice.
10. Every AI-generated response, image, or story is visibly labelled.

## Fredericton pilot

### Pilot name

Fredericton Safe AI Learning Station

### Duration

90 days

### Host options

- Local museum or heritage site
- Public library
- Community centre
- Tourism visitor centre
- Innovation hub
- School or adult-learning program

### Pilot components

- One holographic or projected character station
- Three safe AI learning activities
- Two local story modules
- Staff facilitation guide
- Visitor consent and privacy notice
- Accessibility checklist
- Feedback and evaluation form
- Monthly review report

## Claims discipline

This proposal does not claim that holograms, AI, or the proposed station will
solve museum funding, staffing, attendance, or climate challenges.

The proposal asks a narrower question:

> Can an accessible, staff-supported holographic AI learning station improve
> visitor engagement, staff capacity, cultural interpretation, and community
> participation in a Fredericton pilot?

The answer must come from evidence, not assumption.
