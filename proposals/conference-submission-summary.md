# Conference Submission Summary

## Title

**Safe AI Learning Through Holographic Characters: A Community Prototype for
Museums, Libraries, and Public Spaces**

## Format

Interactive workshop, demonstration, panel, or conference talk.

Preferred format: 45-minute interactive workshop with a live demonstration.

## Audience

Museum professionals, librarians, educators, cultural-sector workers,
accessibility advocates, community organizers, technologists, designers,
policy professionals, students, and researchers.

## One-hundred-word abstract

Museums, libraries, and community spaces face funding pressure, staffing
constraints, digital-capability gaps, and public uncertainty about AI. This
session presents a Fredericton-first prototype: an accessible holographic AI
learning station using clearly fictional characters, local cultural content,
and staff-supported learning activities.

Rather than presenting AI as an authority, the station helps visitors ask
questions, identify errors, protect privacy, and create responsibly. The
session shares a practical safety framework, accessibility approach, cultural
co-design process, evaluation measures, and honest limitations. Participants
will leave with a model for testing safe AI learning in public cultural
spaces.

## Learning outcomes

Participants will be able to:

1. Describe a practical approach to safe, accessible AI learning in museums,
   libraries, and community spaces.
2. Identify safety boundaries for AI characters, including fictional status,
   consent, privacy, source verification, and human escalation.
3. Apply a small-scale evaluation plan before expanding an AI learning pilot.

## Demonstration

The session includes a short demonstration of a holographic or projected AI
learning station.

Participants may see an example interaction with:

- Sparky the Beaver, an AI-literacy guide
- The Memory Keeper, a local-history guide
- The Question Asker, a critical-thinking guide

The demonstration may include one of the following activities:

- Ask the Character
- Spot the Mistake
- Create With AI
- Privacy Mirror
- Accessibility Challenge

## Safety commitments

All AI characters are clearly fictional. They do not claim to be human,
conscious, or an authority.

The demonstration uses:

- Clear AI labels
- Consent before saving or displaying visitor creations
- No required personal accounts
- No impersonation of real people without documented permission
- Source links or references for important historical claims
- Human staff escalation for concerns or accessibility needs
- No medical, legal, or financial advice
- Plain language, captions, large-text options, and audio alternatives where
  available

## Accessibility commitments

The session will use plain-language materials and clear visual design. Where
available, it will include captions, large-text options, audio alternatives,
keyboard-accessible materials, and a quiet or low-stimulation option.

Participants can ask questions, request support, or leave at any time.

## Technical requirements

For an in-person demonstration:

- One table or small display area
- Access to power
- A screen, projector, monitor, or holographic display
- Internet access, if available
- Space for 10–30 participants
- A venue contact for accessibility and technical support

The demonstration can also run offline or with limited connectivity using
pre-approved content.

## Evaluation

Optional anonymous feedback may measure:

- Clarity of the safety framework
- Accessibility of the demonstration
- Usefulness for the participant’s organization
- Interest in a local pilot
- Concerns or missing considerations

No personal data is required.

## Claims discipline

This proposal does not claim that holograms or AI will solve museum funding,
staffing, attendance, or climate challenges.

It proposes a small, measurable pilot to test whether accessible AI learning
can support visitor engagement, staff capacity, cultural interpretation, and
community participation.
